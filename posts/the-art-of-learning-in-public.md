There's a tweet I think about a lot. It goes something like: *"Be so consistent that people think you're a bot."* 

It's meant as productivity advice, but it misses something important. The most interesting people online aren't the ones who never miss a day — they're the ones who are visibly, publicly *becoming*.

## The Comfortable Lie of Private Learning

We have this idea that you should master something before you talk about it. Get the credential, then share. Finish the project, then post about it. Understand the concept completely, then explain it to others.

This is comfortable because it protects you from being wrong in public.

But it also cuts you off from the most powerful feedback loop available: other people.

## What "In Public" Actually Means

Learning in public doesn't mean performing ignorance. It means:

1. Sharing work that's 80% done
2. Asking questions where the answer seems obvious
3. Writing up what you learned *while* you learned it, not 6 months later
4. Being willing to update your views

The last one is underrated. A public update — "I was wrong about X, here's what changed my mind" — builds more trust than a hundred confident posts.

## The Unexpected Gift

Here's what I didn't expect: when you share the messy middle, you find other people in the messy middle. And those connections are qualitatively different from the ones you make when everything is polished.

That's the gift. Not the audience — the community.
