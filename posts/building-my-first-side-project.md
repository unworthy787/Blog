Every developer has a graveyard. Mine has approximately forty-seven projects, most of which died somewhere between "I had a great idea" and "I wrote one function and got distracted."

This is the story of the first project I actually shipped.

## The Graveyard Audit

I did something uncomfortable last year: I went through all my old repos and asked myself why each one died.

The pattern was almost embarrassing in its consistency. Every project that failed had one thing in common: I spent too long on it before showing anyone.

Not because I was being perfectionist — I was genuinely just exploring. But exploration without external feedback is just wandering.

## What I Did Differently

The project I shipped was a simple tool for [organizing reading notes]. Nothing groundbreaking. Here's what changed:

**I told someone about it on day one.** Not to build hype — I literally just mentioned it to a friend over coffee. That tiny act of social accountability changed everything. It made it real.

**I shipped an embarrassing version on week two.** It had one feature. The UI looked like it was built in 2003. I posted it in a Discord server I'm in.

Three people said it was useful.

That was enough.

## The Real Lesson

The lesson isn't "ship fast." That's too simple. The lesson is: *find out if your thing matters before you perfect it.*

Most of my graveyard projects died because I never found out. I kept building, kept polishing, and eventually ran out of energy before reaching the point of external validation.

The tool I shipped was worse. But it was real. And real is what allows you to iterate.
